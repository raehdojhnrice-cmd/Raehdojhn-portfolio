RAE-26-00707Inspiration

Influences

The creative lineage informing the work — disciplines collapse, references compound. A modern renaissance is built on borrowed light.

THESIS

Every body of work I make sits inside a wider conversation. The artists below are the chorus — designers who broke the rules, directors who weaponized atmosphere, photographers who saw beauty in the broken, and painters who refused the limits of their century. I treat their work the way a musician treats a sample library: studied, internalized, and recomposed in service of something new.

METHOD

This is not a list of favorites — it is a working archive. Names appear here because something in the work has been transposed into my practice: a color choice, a typographic instinct, a way of holding a frame, a refusal to flatten contradiction. The list is alphabetical only by accident. The real order is the order I discovered each of them.

PILLAR / 01

Polymathy as Default

Every meaningful body of work in the canon collapses categories. The split between artist and technologist, designer and director, is administrative — not artistic. The list above is built on that conviction.

PILLAR / 02

Reference Without Cosplay

Influence is metabolism, not mimicry. I name these artists because something they made changed how I see — not because I am trying to copy the surface of their work. Borrow the question, not the answer.

PILLAR / 03

Beauty & Brutality

The work I love sits on a knife edge between rigor and rawness. Brutalist architecture, heroin-chic photography, couture made from torn denim — the tension is the point. The grid only matters because something inside it refuses to behave.

PILLAR / 04

The Long Lineage

Da Vinci, Caravaggio, and Michelangelo are on this list with the same weight as Virgil and Tyler. The Renaissance was a network of bodies — patrons, apprentices, rivals — solving for taste in public. We are still in that conversation.
